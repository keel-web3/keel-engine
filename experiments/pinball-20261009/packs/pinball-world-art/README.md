# Pinball machinery and framing art

An optional pack of original machinery, gateways and canonical foliage. Twenty-eight registered objects support the generated pinball machine and its reserved exterior framing. Placement comes entirely from the canonical descriptor; loading the pack adds no scenery to a table. Rails, tracks, flippers, ramps, catches, targets and locks lead the scene through their actual geometry and state.

The generator owns object placement, clear playable footprints, biome weights, layers and physical collision geometry. The pack supplies finite seed variants and role-based paints. Optional machinery and plants occupy deliberately reserved exterior sites, with their full silhouettes outside the ball routes and catch/landing sightlines.

`worldArtAsset(id)` resolves only registered objects. `worldArtPaint(roles, seed, blend)` blends one shared material language through normalized eighth-share biome buckets. Geometry, material and palette-screen/filter selection remain separate. The pack includes inherited foliage profiles so its canonical trees also register correctly through `defineContentPack`.

`planPinballFraming` reserves the full four-variant silhouette and sway envelope outside emitted beds, guides, mouths, shot fans, catch banks and hazards. Its deterministic chunks must be serialized by the compiler and qualified with that world. Cyber ribs, vapor fans, gold buttresses, nature root canopies, crypto diamond chains and nightshift heat exchangers have distinct meshes and material palettes. This art reservation helper cannot approve a physical world or a game.

These are starter blueprints. `definePinballSceneBlueprint` admits a content pack's semantic props, weighted boundary grammar, role-based tones, clipped printed primitives, fixture/tint policy, bounded motion, growth and ambient/music vocabulary. `planPinballSceneGenome(cabinetSeed, stage, blueprint, assets)` retains cabinet identity while deriving the stage stream. The compiler serializes the resulting genome as `ArenaField.scene` before hashing the world. A custom blueprint does not require a starter `theme` name. Additional registered styled objects are supplied to both framing (`assets`) and the view (`worldArtAssets`); their complete four-variant render envelopes are reserved. Unsupported vocabularies and malformed geometry tuples fail admission.

`print.layout:"field"` composes one normalized catch-fan canvas instead of repeating a tile. Contiguous closed primitives of the same material role define quiet substrate panels; open primitives define engraving. Contours must have three distinct corners, nonzero area and no self-crossings. The renderer clips them to each real bed and samples its native plane. Cabinet/stage seed plus stable surface identity supplies bounded layout variation; no frame or draw-order stream affects the artwork. Additional packs author their own semantic layout. An absent layout or `"grid"` preserves the earlier line-grid behavior.

Optional `structure` recipes author native-edge chassis sections and radius-scaled low pivot machinery. Optional `environment` recipes author a recessed scene foundation plus near/mid/far semantic asset tiers. Environment clusters follow actual structural boundaries and stay horizontally outside physical geometry. A bounded native rise places near architecture and light fixtures above its chamber edge; the view cuts only actual posed foreground over the owned body. Other tiers recede below the source deck. They preserve each upper chamber layer and height; cosmetic extents never expand authoritative bounds. Additional content packs may supply their own environment vocabulary and geometry. Regional bindings use the same nearest-center selection as authority and own their framing vocabulary. All recipes and four-variant asset envelopes are bounded and validated by the consumer.

An optional environment `construction` vocabulary defines continuous boundary sections and semantic object mounts using bounded normalized box/ball/capsule parts. Starter profiles have distinct service-gallery, prismatic-parapet, vault, root-bank, cipher-cage and heatworks silhouettes; additional packs author their own grammar. The consumer binds all structural material roles, requires permanent structure, and reserves the complete mount footprint alongside each four-variant object envelope before placement. Every generated socket serializes its real same-layer `anchorSurfaceId`. Native heights and foundations belong to the source geometry; the grammar never lifts a track or changes physics bounds.

Scene blueprint revision8 uses recessed `substrate.layout:"boundary"` for the starter vocabularies. The renderer binds those parts to the largest native catch bed for each actual bank and excludes full part footprints from physical beds/routes. Missing layout retains the legacy lattice grammar for old recipes. The consumer also admits the explicit `lattice` form. New recipes require their own serialized world identity; diagnostic fixtures and source tests do not approve new rectangular map anatomy or thematic pixels.

`planPinballCabinetPrint` and `createPinballCabinetPrint` supply a deterministic lit enamel illustration for each of the six starter themes. The seed changes its composition; bounded16–512 pixel outputs resample the same512 pixel master. Four native ink ramps retain real lighting, and the lower central blade approach stays quiet. These decals belong on the actual chamber surface and do not introduce targets or support planes. Custom scene blueprints retain their own authored art vocabulary.

`createPinballCabinetPrintAsync(options, {yieldControl, signal, maxRowsPerChunk})` produces exactly the synchronous bytes while yielding every4 master/output rows by default. Hosts may choose1–16 rows per chunk. A host can retain its current renderer and warm a separate atlas before swapping resources. Cancelling discards that job. Scheduling and atomic resource installation belong to the host; the row count does not certify a device time budget. The synchronous API still constructs the full master at every detail size, so consumers should use cooperative prewarming for live quality changes.
